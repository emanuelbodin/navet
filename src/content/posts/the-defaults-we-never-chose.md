you wake up inside someone else's config file.

notifications on. recommendations on. location vaguely necessary. the feed already decided what counts as relevant before you finished coffee. none of this was a conversation. it was a **default** — the quiet kind of power that never needs to raise its voice.

## inheritance without consent

society runs on inherited defaults the same way machines do. credit scores. school calendars. the assumption that a phone number equals a person. we call them norms when we like them and bugs when we do not.

machines just make the inheritance faster. and harder to see.

```
$ cat /etc/defaults/human
# edited by committee, 1974-present
# do not change unless you enjoy friction
```

## what to do about it

not much, heroically. a little, daily:

- name the default out loud
- ask who benefits when it stays
- flip one switch that was never yours

navet is for that small act of noticing. society, machines, and the defaults we inherit — especially the ones that feel like weather.
